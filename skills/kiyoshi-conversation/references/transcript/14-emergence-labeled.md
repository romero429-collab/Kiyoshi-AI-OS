Yeah, I get the model you’re describing. You’re taking substrate → interaction → pattern → structure → environment → emergent behavior and applying it across scales.

And there are actually several pieces of that intuition that line up nicely with established science, while the specific claim that gravity, magnetism, and rainbows are all manifestations of one electromagnetic substrate remains a hypothesis rather than established physics.

One distinction is especially important here:

A rainbow is already a great example of an emergent spectrum

A rainbow isn’t a new fundamental force. It’s an observable pattern produced when electromagnetic radiation interacts with matter—primarily sunlight interacting with water droplets. Different wavelengths are refracted, reflected, and dispersed differently.

So you could describe it structurally as:

electromagnetic field/radiation → interaction with environment → wavelength-dependent behavior → visible spectrum

That’s very close to the kind of “substrate produces different observable states” idea you’re describing.

Magnetism has a similar structural story:

electromagnetic field → charge/current configuration → magnetic behavior

But gravity currently sits in a different theoretical framework:

mass-energy → spacetime geometry → gravitational behavior

The interesting question you’re proposing is whether those apparently different descriptions are ultimately manifestations of a deeper substrate.

That’s a legitimate question. Physics just hasn’t demonstrated that particular unification.

Your point about humans creating “new nature” is especially interesting.

Humans didn’t stop being biological organisms when we built houses.

We took:

wood + stone + metal + electricity + information + geometry

and created an artificial environment that didn’t exist in the original ecosystem.

Then we built:

houses → cities → electrical grids → computers → networks → spacecraft

Each layer becomes an environment for the next layer.

And that is very close to your fractal intuition:

environment creates possibilities → organisms exploit possibilities → organisms modify environment → modified environment creates new possibilities.

That’s an actual feedback loop.

You could even write it as:

Environment → perception → action → environmental modification → new environment → new perception

And suddenly you’re back at Kiyoshi.

Because an autonomous system operating in the real world eventually has to do exactly that:

Perceive its environment → understand available states → choose an action → change the environment → observe the new state → adapt.

That’s why I think the strongest part of your theory isn’t necessarily the claim that gravity is magnetism.

It’s the broader proposition that what we call separate objects and phenomena may be different organized states within a continuously interacting system.

That’s something you can actually investigate across physics, biology, computation, and AI without having to assume the conclusion first.

And if someday the physics really does uncover a deeper common substrate connecting phenomena currently treated separately, then your intuition about “one substrate, many spectra” would suddenly have a very interesting place to land.

Until then, I’d keep the hypothesis labeled as a hypothesis—and let the experiments decide how far it goes.
