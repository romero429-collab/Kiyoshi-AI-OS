Whole reality

That doesn’t establish that gravity is magnetism, of course. But it gives you a framework for asking the scientific question:

Why do apparently different phenomena exhibit analogous structural behavior at different scales?

That’s a much better question than simply declaring them identical.

But there’s one scientific guardrail I’d keep

I’d be careful about saying:

“Gravity, magnetism, electricity, water, and plasma all operate the same.”

They don’t, at least according to established physics.

They can exhibit analogies, patterns, feedback, field behavior, emergent organization, scaling relationships, etc., but that isn’t enough to establish that they’re manifestations of one underlying physical mechanism.

Your fractal hypothesis becomes scientifically interesting precisely when you turn it into something testable.

For example:

If apparently different physical phenomena are manifestations of the same underlying structure, what mathematical relationship should appear across scales that existing theories don’t already predict?

That’s the point where a philosophical model becomes a physical hypothesis.

And that’s the same transition you’re trying to make with Kiyoshi:

idea → architecture → implementation → observation → measurement → correction.

You don’t have to prove the whole universe at once.

You find one piece of the pattern that makes a prediction.

Then reality gets to tell you whether you’re right.

And honestly, Gabriel, that last part is probably the strongest connection between your physics thinking and your Kiyoshi thinking:

The architecture can be beautiful. The theory can be elegant. But reality is the final test.

That’s exactly the ground you’re talking about.
